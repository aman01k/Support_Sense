# SupportSense — Viva Questions & Answers

Short answers you can say out loud. ⭐ = very likely to be asked.

---

## A. The project

**⭐ 1. Explain your project in one minute.**
SupportSense reads a customer support message and predicts three things: the **category** of the problem (like Refund or Payment), the customer's **sentiment**, and the **urgency**. Category uses a machine learning model, TF-IDF with Logistic Regression, trained on 19,000 labelled messages and 99.6% accurate on unseen data. Sentiment uses VADER, a pre-trained analyser. Urgency uses business rules based on keywords and sentiment. Everything is shown in a Streamlit dashboard with the most urgent tickets first.

**2. What problem does it solve?**
Manually reading and sorting thousands of tickets is slow, and urgent issues like fraud or double charges can get lost in the queue. Our system does the first sorting automatically.

**3. Is this a chatbot?**
No. A chatbot replies to customers. Our system **classifies and prioritises** tickets for the support team.

**4. Which NLP tasks does it use?**
Text classification (category), sentiment analysis, and rule-based information extraction (urgency keywords).

## B. Dataset

**⭐ 6. Which dataset did you use and why?**
The Bitext Customer Support dataset from Hugging Face: 26,872 English messages with 11 category labels. It has no missing values, the categories match real support teams, and it's clean enough for a reliable model.

**7. Did you consider other datasets?**
Yes, the Tobi-Bueck support tickets dataset, which has priority labels. But half of it is German and its labels are noisy: our baseline got only 52% for department and 62% for priority. One ticket titled "Urgent Help Needed for Possible Data Breach" is labelled *medium* priority.

**8. What are the 11 categories?**
ACCOUNT, ORDER, REFUND, INVOICE, CONTACT, PAYMENT, FEEDBACK, DELIVERY, SHIPPING, SUBSCRIPTION, CANCEL.

**9. Is the dataset balanced?**
No. ACCOUNT has about 6,000 examples and CANCEL about 950. We handled it with a stratified split, `class_weight="balanced"`, and macro F1.

**10. Is the data real?**
It's synthetic, generated by Bitext from templates with added typos and slang. That's why accuracy is so high, and it's our main limitation.

---

## C. Preprocessing

**⭐ 11. What preprocessing did you do?**
Lowercasing, removing placeholders like `{{Order Number}}`, removing numbers like `#12588`, and collapsing extra spaces. Then we removed 3,096 duplicates.

**⭐ 12. Why didn't you remove stop words?**
Stop-word lists include "not", "no" and "can't". *"I can pay"* and *"I can't pay"* mean opposite things, so removing them would hurt.

**13. Why no stemming or lemmatization?**
Messages are short, and TF-IDF with word pairs already works very well. Fewer steps also means it's easier to explain and debug.

**⭐ 14. Why remove duplicates?**
If the same sentence appears in both training and test data, the model is tested on something it memorised. That inflates the score, which is a form of **data leakage**.

**15. What is a stratified split?**
A split where each category keeps the same percentage in train and test. CANCEL is 4% of both. Without it, a small class could end up under-represented in the test set.

**16. Why 80/20?**
It's the standard choice. 19,020 messages is plenty to learn from, and 4,756 is enough for a reliable test.

---

## D. TF-IDF

**⭐ 17. What is TF-IDF?**
Term Frequency × Inverse Document Frequency. It turns text into numbers. A word gets a high score if it appears often in this message but rarely across all messages. "Refund" scores high and is informative; "the" scores low because it's everywhere.

**18. Formula?**
TF-IDF = TF(word, doc) × log(N / DF(word)), where N is the number of documents and DF is the number of documents containing the word.

**19. Why TF-IDF and not Bag of Words?**
Bag of Words only counts words, so common words like "I" and "my" get high values. TF-IDF down-weights them automatically.

**20. What does `ngram_range=(1,2)` do?**
It uses single words and two-word phrases, so "payment methods" and "not working" become features. This captures some word order and negation.

**21. How many features?**
4,823, after ignoring terms that appear in only one message (`min_df=2`).

**⭐ 22. Why fit TF-IDF only on training data?**
If we fitted it on all the data, the vocabulary and IDF values would include information from the test set. That's data leakage. Our Pipeline guarantees TF-IDF is fitted on training data only.

## E. Model

**⭐ 23. Why Logistic Regression?**
It's fast, works very well on TF-IDF text features, is easy to explain, and gives **probabilities**, which we use for the confidence check.

**24. How does Logistic Regression work for 11 classes?**
It learns a weight for every word for every category. For a new message it adds up the weights per category, and softmax turns those scores into probabilities that sum to 1. The highest probability wins.

**⭐ 25. You compared it with SVM. Why not use SVM, which scored higher?**
The SVM was 99.8% vs 99.6%, about 10 messages out of 4,756. But Linear SVM doesn't give probabilities, and we need them to flag uncertain tickets. The tiny accuracy gain wasn't worth losing that.

**26. What does `class_weight="balanced"` do?**
It makes errors on small classes (CANCEL) cost more, so the model doesn't ignore them in favour of big classes (ACCOUNT).

**⭐ 27. Why not BERT or deep learning?**
We already get 99.6% with a model that trains in seconds and is fully explainable. BERT needs a GPU, is slower and harder to explain, and has nothing to improve on this data. It would make sense for messier real tickets.

**28. How is the model saved and used?**
The TF-IDF + Logistic Regression Pipeline is saved with `joblib` as `category_model.joblib`. The app loads it once at start-up.

**29. What did the model learn?**
The top words per category make sense. For REFUND: refund, reimbursement, money, rebate. For CONTACT: agent, person, speak. That shows it learned real signals, not noise.

## F. Evaluation

**⭐ 30. What are your results?**
99.6% accuracy and 0.997 macro F1 on 4,756 unseen messages. Every category has precision and recall of at least 0.987. Only 17 mistakes, mostly heavy typos.

**⭐ 31. 99.6% seems too good. Is something wrong?**
We checked for leakage: duplicates were removed and TF-IDF was fitted on training data only. The real reason is that Bitext is template-generated, so wording within each category is very consistent. On real tickets accuracy would be lower. That's why we added the confidence check.

**⭐ 32. Explain precision, recall and F1.**
- **Precision:** of the tickets we predicted as REFUND, how many really were REFUND.
- **Recall:** of all the real REFUND tickets, how many we found.
- **F1:** the harmonic mean of the two. It's high only if both are high.

**33. Why macro F1 and not just accuracy?**
Accuracy can look great while a small class does badly. Macro F1 averages F1 over the 11 categories equally, so CANCEL counts as much as ACCOUNT.

**34. What does the confusion matrix show?**
Rows are true categories, columns are predictions. Almost everything is on the diagonal (correct). The few errors are between ORDER, PAYMENT and ACCOUNT, which share words like "order", "account" and "help".

**⭐ 35. What is the confidence check?**
If the model's top probability is below 50%, the ticket is marked "Needs manual review". On the test set, 74 tickets were flagged. Accuracy is 99.9% on confident tickets and 82.4% on flagged ones, so the flag correctly picks out the risky predictions.

## G. Sentiment

**⭐ 36. How does sentiment analysis work?**
We use VADER, a pre-trained rule-based analyser. It has a dictionary of about 7,500 words scored for positive or negative feeling, plus rules for negation ("not good"), intensifiers ("extremely"), CAPITALS and "!!!". It outputs a compound score from −1 to +1.

**⭐ 37. Why not train a sentiment model?**
Our dataset has no sentiment labels. Training would mean manually labelling thousands of messages. VADER needs no training and works well on short informal text.

**38. Why is your Positive threshold 0.5 instead of VADER's usual 0.05?**
Support messages are polite. "Please tell me how to update my address" scores +0.32 just because of "please", but the customer isn't happy, just polite. A higher bar keeps such messages Neutral.

**39. How accurate is it?**
94% on 36 hand-labelled messages. It missed "overcharged", which isn't in VADER's dictionary, and "Please refund me immediately or I will report you", where "please" cancelled out the threat.

## H. Urgency

**⭐ 40. How do you detect urgency?**
With rules checked in order:
1. Security, money, legal or "urgent" keywords → High.
2. Very negative tone (≤ −0.7) → High.
3. Problem words like "not working" or "error" → Medium.
4. Any negative tone → Medium.
5. Everything else → Low.

Each result shows its reason, like "money: charged twice".

**⭐ 41. Why rules and not ML for urgency?**
There are no reliable urgency labels. The one dataset with priorities had noisy labels and only 62% accuracy. Rules need no data, are transparent, and are easy for a business to change.

**42. Why combine keywords and sentiment?**
Keywords alone miss furious customers who use no keyword. Sentiment alone misses calm-but-serious messages like "I need access to my account urgently". Together they cover both.

**⭐ 43. Which urgency metric matters most?**
**Recall for High.** Missing a real urgent ticket like fraud is much worse than wrongly flagging a normal one. Our High recall is 100% on the test messages.

**44. Did you change the rules after testing?**
Yes. "I chose a wrong address by mistake" scored −0.67 and was marked High, which is too strict, so we moved the very-negative threshold from −0.6 to −0.7. We also moved "without my permission" from the money group to the security group.

## I. Dashboard & system

**45. Why Streamlit?**
It's pure Python with no HTML or JavaScript, so we could build a working interface for the model quickly.

**46. What does the dashboard show?**
A box to analyse a message, cards for total, High-priority, % negative and needs-review counts, charts by category, urgency and sentiment, and a filterable table sorted High urgency first.

**47. Where are tickets stored?**
In a CSV file, `data/tickets.csv`. It's simple, needs no setup, and is enough for a demo. A real deployment would use a database like PostgreSQL.

**48. What happens with empty input?**
`analyze_ticket()` rejects anything under 2 words with a friendly message. We tested empty, spaces, one word, None, emoji, HTML, non-English and a 600-word message: 0 crashes.

**⭐ 49. Walk me through what happens when I type a message.**
The text goes three ways:
1. It's cleaned, turned into TF-IDF numbers and classified by Logistic Regression, giving a category and confidence.
2. VADER scores it, giving the sentiment.
3. The urgency rules check keywords and the sentiment score, giving the urgency and reason.

The result is saved to the CSV and appears in the dashboard.

**50. Why does VADER use the raw text but the classifier uses cleaned text?**
VADER needs capitals and "!!!" to judge intensity, and cleaning would remove those. The classifier needs consistent lowercase text that matches its training data.

## J. Limitations & improvements

**⭐ 51. What are the limitations?**
1. Synthetic training data, so real accuracy will be lower.
2. Only one category per ticket.
3. The sentiment and urgency test set is small and we wrote it ourselves.
4. The keyword rules ignore negation: "I was **not** charged twice" → High.
5. VADER misses sarcasm: "Great, another late delivery" → Positive.
6. English only.

**52. Show me a case where it fails.**
"My package hasn't arrived yet and it has been 10 days" is predicted ACCOUNT, which is wrong. But confidence is only 28%, so it's flagged for review. Bitext's delivery messages are questions like "how long does delivery take", not complaints.

**⭐ 53. How would you improve it?**
1. Train on real company tickets.
2. Collect agent-assigned priorities and train a real urgency classifier.
3. Try DistilBERT if real data is messier.
4. Support multiple categories per ticket.
5. Handle negation in the urgency rules.
6. Use a database and add automatic team assignment.

**54. How would this work in a real company?**
New emails or form submissions would be sent to `analyze_ticket()` automatically, perhaps through an API. Tickets would go to the right team by category, and High-urgency ones would trigger an alert. Agents would review flagged tickets, and their corrections would become new training data.
